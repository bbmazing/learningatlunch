Slide 2 — LLM: Large Language Models
  • LLM = Large Language Model. AI trained on huge amounts of text.
  • Every logo is an LLM, or a product built on one.
  • ChatGPT is the app. GPT-5 is the engine inside, like a car and its engine.
  • Most people use the free, standard model. The paid tiers run stronger ones.
  • Ask: who used one this week? Most hands go up.

Slide 3 — OCBC Indonesia: On-premise
  • Public AI runs in someone else’s cloud. Confidential bank data must not go there.
  • So OCBC Indonesia went on-premise. We bought GPUs to host multiple open-source models. One of them is Qwen 3.6.
  • Use the in-house built applications. Your prompts and documents stay inside the bank.
  • Every demo you will see today runs on this setup.

Slide 4 — The premise
  • Everyone arrives with questions: will it replace us, can it help my work, where do I start?
  • For us this is not a question. AI already works in this bank.
  • Goal today: understand it, watch it work, leave with ideas for your desk.

Slide 6 — Part 1 — What is inside an LLM?
  • Time to open the box.
  • One idea explains everything: the magic and the mistakes.

Slide 7 — Autocomplete, on an enormous scale
  • An LLM does one thing: guess the next word.
  • Your phone keyboard is a tiny version of this.
  • It adds a word, then guesses again, until the answer is done.
  • Hold this picture. It explains the failures coming next.

Slide 8 — It reads tokens, not words
  • The model does not read words. It reads tokens, small pieces of text.
  • Watch the sentence get cut into nine pieces. A short word is one token.
  • A long word breaks into pieces, like menguntungkan into three.
  • Rule of thumb: 1,000 tokens is about 750 English words.
  • So next-word prediction is really next-token prediction. Same idea.

Slide 9 — Tokens are the meter
  • Every token in and out gets counted. Tokens are the meter.
  • Read the receipt: a short email is a few hundred tokens. A 40-page report is tens of thousands.
  • A stronger model multiplies the price of every token.
  • That is why ChatGPT Plus costs 20 US dollars a month, and why companies pay per token.
  • Nothing to do about it today. Just know that AI work has a price tag.

Slide 10 — It read a lot, up to a date
  • It learned by reading books, articles, code and forums. A huge library.
  • Training stops at a date. After that date, the model has read nothing.
  • Ask about last week and it may guess. Fluent about the past, blind to the present.
  • Remember this. Part 2 shows how tools feed it fresh information.

Slide 11 — Brilliant and unreliable at the same time
  • Good at rewriting language: draft, summarise, translate, explain.
  • Weak at fresh news, exact math, and anything about our customers.
  • Hallucination: it can invent a rule and cite it with full confidence.
  • Rule: every fact it gives is a draft. Check before it travels.

Slide 12 — Context in, quality out
  • Better input, better output.
  • Left: one vague line, generic answer. Right: role, context, tone. Ready to edit.
  • Think of it as a smart intern on day one. Brief it like one.

Slide 13 — A plain chatbot hits three walls
  • Wall one, time: its knowledge stops at a cutoff date.
  • Wall two, access: it cannot see our data or systems.
  • Wall three, action: it writes text. It cannot do anything.
  • Part 2 is how we break all three.

Slide 14 — Part 2 — From chatbot to coworker
  • Part two: how a text predictor starts doing real work.
  • Three ideas: tools, agents, grounding. Every demo uses them.

Slide 15 — Tool calling: give the model hands
  • Give the model tools: search, calculator, database.
  • Ask for today’s rate: it calls the rate tool, then answers with the live number.
  • The model thinks and writes. Tools fetch facts and act.
  • This one idea powers all four demos.

Slide 16 — An agent is a loop, not a chat
  • An agent is an LLM in a loop: plan, act, check, repeat until done.
  • You give it a goal, not a question.
  • Screening a company for negative news is exactly this loop.
  • You will watch it live in Demo 2.

Slide 17 — Ground it in your documents
  • Fix for wall two: hand it your documents when you ask.
  • It answers only from those pages, and cites them.
  • If the answer is not there, it says so. No inventing.
  • Lumina, demo four, runs on this.

Slide 18 — Part 3 — Built here
  • Now it gets concrete. Four tools, four videos, all built here.
  • For each: the pain, the video, the lesson. Questions after each.

Slide 19 — Bank Statement Analyzer
  • The pain: statements from other banks, every format different, retyped by hand.
  • The tool: OCR reads the pages. The model builds clean tables.
  • The analyst still decides. The retyping is gone.

Slide 20 — Adverse News Search · 1:45
  • The pain: screening by hand. One name, one source, one search at a time.
  • Watch: it writes its own searches. Courts, negative news, sanction lists.
  • Verdict from credible sources only. Both names: high risk, same KPK case.
  • One click: a PDF report, ready for the KYC file.
  • The agent does the legwork. The officer makes the call.

Slide 21 — MoM AI · 0:30
  • The pain: someone types notes all meeting, and the minutes still come late.
  • Drop in the recording. It writes out who said what.
  • Out come minutes: summary, attendees, actions with names and dates.
  • English or Bahasa. Export to PDF or Word.
  • Thirty minutes of typing becomes five minutes of checking.

Slide 22 — LuminaLM · 0:50
  • The pain: 157 documents, and the truth scattered across all of them.
  • All files become one wiki. Ask anything: the answer cites its source page.
  • If the answer is not in the files, it says so.
  • Studio drafts the weekly Word, Excel and PowerPoint packs.
  • A colleague who read every document and never forgets a page.

Slide 23 — Four tools, one pattern
  • Same pattern four times: one model, plus tools, plus our documents.
  • The AI removes the reading, typing and searching.
  • The judgment stays human: approve, clear, sign, decide.

Slide 24 — Start this week, inside the rules
  • Start small: summarise, draft, translate, explain.
  • Rule one: no customer data in public AI tools. Use the bank’s own.
  • Rule two: check every fact and number.
  • Rule three: it is a draft. What you send carries your name.
