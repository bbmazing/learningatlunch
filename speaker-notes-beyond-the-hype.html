Beyond the Hype What AI Can Do Today

  • Welcome. I am Bryan, from Analytics & Decision Making.

Slide 2

LLM: Large Language Models

  • LLM = Large Language Model. AI trained on huge amounts of text.
  • Every logo is an LLM, or a product built on one.
  • ChatGPT is the app. GPT-5 is the engine inside, like a car and its engine.
  • Most people use the free, standard model. The paid tiers run stronger ones.
  • Ask: who used one this week? Most hands go up.
Slide 3

OCBC Indonesia: On-premise

  • Public AI runs in someone else’s cloud. Confidential bank data must not go there.
  • So OCBC Indonesia went on-premise. We bought GPUs to host multiple open-source models. One of them is Qwen 3.6.
  • Use the in-house built applications. Your prompts and documents stay inside the bank.
  • Every demo you will see today runs on this setup.

Slide 6

Part 1 — What is inside an LLM?

  • Time to open the box.
  • One idea explains everything: the magic and the mistakes.
Slide 7

Autocomplete, on an enormous scale

  • An LLM does one thing: guess the next word.
  • Your phone keyboard is a tiny version of this.
  • It adds a word, then guesses again, until the answer is done.
  • Small confession before we move on: word is not exactly the right unit.
  • Hold this picture. The next slide sharpens it.
Slide 8

The correction: tokens, not words

  • On the last slide I kept saying words. Here is the correction: the real unit is the token.
  • Follow the pipeline. Step one: the sentence you type.
  • Step two: it gets cut into tokens, numbered pieces from the model’s dictionary of about 100,000 entries. Short word, one piece. A long word like menguntungkan becomes three.
  • Step three: what the model actually receives. No letters. A list of numbers, and inside, each number becomes thousands of numbers. Predicting is math on those.
  • Rule of thumb: 1,000 tokens is about 750 English words. Next-word prediction is really next-token prediction.
Slide 9

Tokens are the meter

  • Every token in and out gets counted. Tokens are the meter.
  • Read the receipt: a short email is a few hundred tokens. A 40-page report is tens of thousands.
  • For consumers it is a subscription: free, or 20 dollars a month for ChatGPT Plus.
  • For companies it is per token. Our self-hosted Qwen 3.6 works out around 3 dollars per million tokens, mostly the GPU hosting cost. GPT-5.5, the best GPT today, charges 30 dollars per million output tokens. Ten times ours.
  • That is why the bank sizes the model to the task. Nothing for you to do. Just know AI has a price tag.
Slide 10

It read a lot, up to a date

  • It learned by reading books, articles, code and forums. A huge library.
  • Training stops at a date. After that date, the model has read nothing.
  • Ask about last week and it may guess. Fluent about the past, blind to the present.
  • Remember this. Part 2 shows how tools feed it fresh information.
Slide 11

What it does brilliantly

  • Good news first. Anything shaped like language work, it does well.
  • Four everyday wins: draft, summarise, translate, explain.
  • Each card carries a starter prompt you can copy this afternoon.
  • If the task is language in, language out, it is probably good at it.
Slide 12

The limits, and their fixes

  • Now the limits. Four big ones.
  • No news after its training date. Weak at exact math. Blind to our data. And it can invent facts with confidence, like citing an OJK rule that does not exist.
  • Key message: every limit has a fix, and most fixes are tools. Web search, a calculator, your documents.
  • Hold that thought. Part 2 is exactly this.
Slide 13

Be clear about what you want

  • Before the tools: how to ask well. Clarity beats everything.
  • Watch the transformation. The vague prompt becomes a clear one by adding three things.
  • The task, exactly. The background it cannot know. The format, tone and length.
  • Not happy with the answer? Refine and ask again. It never gets tired.
Slide 14

Or use a framework: COSTAR

  • Or use a framework. The one I like is COSTAR.
  • Read it down the left edge: Context, Objective, Style, Tone, Audience, Response.
  • Six boxes to fill before you press enter. Skip what you do not need.
  • Next slide: a real one, filled in.
Slide 15

COSTAR, filled in

  • New scenario: generating a report. This is where unwanted extras and hallucination hurt, so we fence them in.
  • The R box does the heavy work: exact columns, only from my notes, N/A for missing data. No adding, no inventing.
  • Right: the table it produced. Five columns as ordered, and look at the last row: N/A instead of a made-up answer.
  • Specify the shape and the rules, and you get the report you wanted. Copy this shape.
Slide 16

You will never be good at it unless you try

  • One more thing before the tools: practice. You will never be good at this unless you try.
  • We have our own internal ChatGPT: Chat OCBC. It runs inside the bank, on our Qwen.
  • For daily work it is already enough: summaries, drafts, translations, explanations, ideas.
  • Open it after this session. Try the starter prompts from today. Bookmark it: this is your training ground.
Slide 17

Three walls still stand between chat and real work

  • Before part two, look at where we stand. Language work: strong, and you have a place to practise.
  • But three walls keep a chatbot from real bank work. Frozen in time. Blind to our data. All talk.
  • One fix breaks all three: give the model tools.
  • That is Part 2. Let us go.
Slide 18

Part 2 — From chatbot to coworker

  • Part two: the fixes we promised.
  • Four ideas: tools, agents, grounding, and a human in the loop.
  • Every use case in part three runs on them.
Slide 19

Tool calling: give the model hands

  • Remember the fixes we promised on the limits slide: web search, a calculator, our documents. They are all the same idea: tools.
  • Ask for the dollar rate. The model knows its training data is stale, so it calls the rate tool and answers with the live number.
  • The model thinks and writes. Tools fetch facts and act.
  • This one idea powers every use case coming up.
Slide 20

An agent is a loop, not a chat

  • An agent is an LLM in a loop: plan, act, check, repeat until done.
  • You give it a goal, not a question.
  • Screening a company for negative news is exactly this loop.
  • You will see it in the Adverse News use case.
Slide 21

Ground it in your documents

  • Fix for wall two: hand it your documents when you ask.
  • It answers only from those pages, and cites them.
  • If the answer is not there, it says so. No inventing.
  • LuminaLM, use case four, runs on this.
Slide 22

The machine drafts. You verify.

  • The fourth idea is not a technology. It is a rule: keep a human in the loop.
  • Example: OCR reads a transfer instruction. Fast and usually right. But look at the flags.
  • Before anything moves, a person double-checks the fields that matter: account number, name, amount.
  • AI reads fast. You confirm what moves money. This checkpoint appears in every use case next.
Slide 23

Part 3 — Built here

  • Now it gets concrete. Four use cases, all built here.
  • Two I will walk through step by step. Two you will watch on video.
  • For each: the pain, how it works, and where the human stays in the loop.
Slide 24

Bank Statement Analyzer

  • Use case one, and a pain every credit and ops person knows: statements from other banks arrive as PDFs and phone scans, and someone retypes them for hours.
  • Walk the flow. OCR reads every line and a fraud check flags signs of tampering.
  • Then the checkpoint from the last slide: a person verifies the fields that matter. Account numbers, names, totals.
  • The model categorises every transaction: who was paid, what type, business or personal.
  • Everything lands in one dashboard: money in, money out, account behaviour. Hours of retyping become minutes of checking.
Slide 25

Adverse News Search

  • Use case two: screening. Before we deal with a company or a person, we check for negative news. By hand that means dozens of searches, one source at a time.
  • Walk the flow. Names go in: a company and its related parties.
  • The agent writes its own searches: court databases like SIPP and Mahkamah Agung, negative-news keywords, sanction and PEP lists.
  • It reads the results and filters: credible sources only, same-name noise dropped. Then a verdict with a written narrative, every claim cited.
  • Last step, the checkpoint again: the officer reviews and clears the name, and a PDF report goes to the KYC file. An hour of googling becomes minutes of review.
Slide 26

MoM AI · 0:30

  • The pain: someone types notes all meeting, and the minutes still come late.
  • Drop in the recording. It writes out who said what.
  • Out come minutes: summary, attendees, actions with names and dates.
  • English or Bahasa. Export to PDF or Word.
  • Thirty minutes of typing becomes five minutes of checking.
Slide 27

LuminaLM · 0:50

  • The pain: 157 documents, and the truth scattered across all of them.
  • All files become one wiki. Ask anything: the answer cites its source page.
  • If the answer is not in the files, it says so.
  • Studio drafts the weekly Word, Excel and PowerPoint packs.
  • A colleague who read every document and never forgets a page.
Slide 28

Four tools, one pattern

  • Same pattern four times: one model, plus tools, plus our documents.
  • The AI removes the reading, typing and searching.
  • The judgment stays human: approve, clear, sign, decide.
